Highlights

At a glance

Ubuntu Sans and its monospaced companion, Ubuntu Sans Mono, form an open-source humanist sans-serif family designed for screen clarity. The two branches are intended to work together, not as separate voices: they share a consistent rhythm, compatible tone, and recognisable character details that keep the family distinctive without compromising legibility.

Origins of Ubuntu: Ubuntu (2010)

Ubuntu’s first release was crafted by Dalton Maag; among the team was Vincent Connare, known for Comic Sans and Trebuchet MS.

As in many typeface projects, the design process began with four characters – n, o, H, O – which established the proportions, curves, and rhythm that guided the remaining 80% of the character set. The result was a typeface that balanced friendliness with professionalism, distinctive enough to carry a brand yet neutral enough for extended reading.

The font’s impact was recognised by London’s Design Museum, which hosted the “Shape My Language” exhibition in early 2011 – a rare honour for a typeface, and rarer still for one released under an open licence.

The 2023 update: Ubuntu Sans

In 2023, Canonical commissioned Type Network, DJR, and Dual Type to expand the typeface for use in design systems. The updated family was renamed Ubuntu Sans to reflect its maturity and expanded capabilities.

The update introduces variable font axes for weight and width. But Ubuntu Sans goes further: it includes true small caps and text figures, features vanishingly rare among open-source fonts.

Improved italics

The original Ubuntu italic was set with tight tracking, a design choice that drew criticism for reducing readability at smaller sizes.

The 2023 update opens up the spacing, allowing the italic to breathe and improving legibility across paragraphs of text.

{% set small_caps_description %}

Ubuntu Sans includes true small caps – not scaled-down capitals, but glyphs designed from scratch to harmonise with lowercase letterforms.

Small caps matter for acronyms, legal text, and subtle emphasis – yet most typefaces, free or commercial, simply omit them. True small caps are drawn to blend with lowercase, a mark of typographic maturity.

Alongside small caps come text figures (also known as oldstyle numerals) – numbers designed to sit comfortably within running text rather than standing at attention like tabular figures.

{% set mono_description %}

The 2010 Ubuntu Mono was a capable fixed-width companion, but its metrics didn’t quite align with its proportional sibling. The x-height sat lower; the stroke weight appeared lighter.

The 2023 update brought Ubuntu Sans Mono into alignment. When proportional and monospaced text appear together – in documentation, code samples, or UI – they share a consistent visual weight and rhythm.
