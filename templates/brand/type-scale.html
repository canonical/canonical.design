Type scale

Our type scale is based on Spencer Mortensen's refinement of the classical modular scale described by Robert Bringhurst in The Elements of Typographic Style¹ – a system with centuries of proven application in both print and digital typography.

Mortensen replaces the classical scale's finite sequence with a continuous formula that identifies and corrects several outliers in the traditional progression. Because his improvements deliver greater mathematical precision – a quality central to our brand – we adopt his sequence as the basis for all three tier scales.

The scale is expressed as a fractional-step geometric progression:

f(i) = f(0) × pow(ratio, i / n)

`f(0)` is the base type size `ratio` defines the harmonic relationship – 2 for octave-based scales `n` represents the number of steps per octave – 5 in Mortensen's classic scale `i` is the position in the sequence

The result is an infinite sequence of harmonically related sizes. Each tier selects a contiguous subset of this sequence for its heading hierarchy.

Sites: scannability and clarity

Marketing sites, blog posts, and promotional materials – serve users who are scanning, evaluating, and consuming information in short, focused sessions. In this context, higher contrast and more generous spacing facilitate quick comprehension and help users rapidly locate the information they need.

Our editorial typography applies strong contrasts in type size to establish a clear visual hierarchy that supports rapid content scanning.

Base size

We adhere to the browser default of 1rem (16px), controllable through browser settings. For simplicity, all values further down are expressed in px, although in the actual code they are in rem.

Rationale

Visitors are scanning and evaluating; generous contrast and hierarchy support rapid comprehension.

Scale

Three paired size steps. H1/H2 at 42px (500/300), H3/H4 at 24px (500/300), H5/H6 at 16px (both 600); body at 16px (400).

Responsive behaviour

H1/H2 and H3/H4 scale at breakpoints; body, H5, H6 stay at 16px (accessibility floor).

Documentation: information density with semantic clarity

Documentation typography prioritizes information density while maintaining the clarity and semantic hierarchy needed for focused, technical reading.

Our technical writing typesets hierarchies that support scanning and semantic emphasis without adding visual noise to dense content.

Base size

Documentation uses a 14px base size (0.875rem), optimized for information density while maintaining comfortable reading.

Rationale

Information density is essential, but must not obscure semantic hierarchy. A restrained scale keeps long technical content easy to scan.

Scale

Three paired size steps. H1/H2 at 32px (500/700 and 300/500), H3/H4 at 24px (500/700 and 300/500), H5/H6 at 18px (400/600); body at 14px (400/600).

Responsive behaviour

H1/H2 step down on smaller screens; H3–H6 and body retain their sizes so semantic hierarchy and reading density remain predictable.

Weight

Medium weights mark headings while stronger bolds remain available for semantic emphasis within body copy.

Emphasis model

Emphasis is reserved for strong semantic meaning rather than decorative hierarchy, making important terms and relationships identifiable without disrupting reading flow.

Applications: information density

Applications support focused, information-dense interaction. Their typography must preserve hierarchy without consuming space needed for tools, data, and controls.

Our application scale increases density while retaining enough contrast between interface levels for confident scanning.

Base size

Applications use a 14px base size (0.875rem) to support information density and frequent, focused interaction.

Rationale

Compact type increases information density while allowing task context, controls, and content to coexist in a constrained interface.

Scale

Three paired size steps. H1/H2 at 24px (500/300), H3/H4 at 18px (600/400), H5/H6 at 14px (both 600); body at 14px (400).

Responsive behaviour

Scale remains deliberately compact; responsive layouts reflow content rather than reducing type below the 14px accessibility floor.
